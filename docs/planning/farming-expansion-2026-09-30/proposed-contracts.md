# Proposed contracts for review

These are recommended prototype defaults, not implemented gameplay or measured final balance. They close the design gaps sufficiently to review a first implementation. The remaining choices are grouped in the executive overview; measurements can change prices and yields without changing the bounded loop.

## Seeds: fresh discoveries from plant drop pools

**User decisions:** seed packs will be added to plant drop pools; Alter Echo retirement provides no compensation. Packs are fresh discoveries for everyone, including legacy players. Old crop unlocks grant or reveal no packs. The previous all-gathering work counter, guaranteed selected pack, pending entitlement, five-minute token refill and twice-capacity storage proposal are superseded. There is no approved guarantee, pity timer or seed inventory cap.

Recommended first-release design for review: a crop-specific pack pays for one finite bed/batch. Recipe availability is computed from new pack discovery plus current game-authored level/quest requirements; subsequent packs sustain planting. Do not serialize a second recipe-unlocked flag or grandfather old crop access. This gives advanced players a new farm-recipe collection even when their adventure crop discoveries/levels are already complete. Fresh pack discovery and no grandfathering are decisions; the exact recipe requirements remain a recommendation for review. If unlock means new varieties, bed capacity or seed tiers instead, that requires a different progression table and verified art; do not invent those additions.

Actual `TaskData.resourceDrops` use `ResourceDrop` entries pointing to Resource assets. `DropResolver.RollDrops` performs one weighted guaranteed selection, removes that entry, then considers sequential additional slots, stopping at the first failed chance. Radish currently has one crop entry and no additional slots. Adding a seed entry to that same list therefore competes with Radish for its guaranteed slot; merely appending an extra-slot chance does not guarantee the crop because the first slot can still pick seeds. [DropResolver](../../../Assets/Scripts/Upgrades/DropResolver.cs), [Radish TaskData](../../../Assets/Resources/Tasks/Farming/Radish.asset).

Recommend a separately rolled seed sub-pool attached to the existing adventure plant reward definition, after the unchanged crop roll. That keeps plant drops as the acquisition source without accidentally reducing existing quest crop supply. A literal shared weighted pool is an alternative if the user wants seeds to substitute for crops; measure the resulting crop-loss percentage explicitly. Either choice needs new authored seed entries and a typed reward adapter. `ResourceGeneratingTask.GenerateDrops` multiplies every normal drop by skill milestones, global/resource bonuses, buffs and resonance before `ResourceManager.Add`; that Add can then apply resource-tier yield. [Reward seam](../../../Assets/Scripts/Tasks/ResourceGeneratingTask.cs). Reusing ordinary Resource grants unaltered would expose seeds to resource-tier bonuses, BestPerMinute, stew, Alter Echo eligibility and generic inventory UI. Prefer a separate integer seed balance keyed by stable recipe ID with a seed-aware drop result. An ordinary Resource representation is viable only with explicit opt-outs and tested routing; no Seed Resource assets exist today.

Proposed eligible source is completed adventure FarmingTask plant rewards. Logging, mining, fishing, quests, town plants and farm harvest do not generate these drops. Whether Echo performers also roll seeds remains a decision; recommend primary hero only initially, with attribution captured before TaskController clears the claim. Death/abandonment retain seed rewards already committed from completed tasks, without an ending bonus. Incomplete tasks yield no seeds. Duplicate task callbacks cannot reroll or re-award. Persist the rolled result/reward identity with balance changes; interruption must preserve the ordinary reward contract and never replay a random draw after a committed grant.

There is no starter grant in the current proposal: it is withdrawn following the fresh-discovery decision. All seed balances start zero/undiscovered, regardless of legacy crop Earned, skill level, cards, totalReceived or quest history. Discovery is derived only from cumulative durably committed new-version pack acquisitions and persists when the balance reaches zero; later reloads/upgrades preserve that new seed record. Recommend first discovery from the approved plant-drop source, without inventing an exceptional tutorial award. Radish is level 1; Corn is level 7. The zero-seed route is to complete level 1 adventure Radish plants until a usable pack drops, return and plant; the rest of the game remains available while the farm waits.

Store quantity plus cumulative lifetimeAcquired; derive discovery from lifetimeAcquired > 0, with no extra saved discovery/recipe/plot-unlocked flag. Use existing resource conventions: shared unknown packet, “Undiscovered”/“???”, no crop name/icon until its new pack is found. Do not derive discovery from the old crop resource. The exact authored recipe/level requirements remain a recommended recipe rule, not approval of specific drop weights or pack amounts. [Verified packet/unknown UI evidence](seed-discovery-and-art.md) supports one common unknown icon. Generic journal question-mark presentation already works; inventory requires an authored shared UnknownIcon or an explicit new seed presenter.

A random pool with nonzero useful probability has no finite guaranteed wait. Do not promise “at most five minutes” or an ordinary-return guarantee. For independent per-completion useful probability p, no useful pack after n completions has probability (1-p)^n; at illustrative p=0.05,59 completions give about 95% acquisition and90about99%, not a guarantee or measured run duration.

Decisions needed before content authoring: bonus sub-pool versus substitution; per-plant matching packs versus an eligible global crop pool; the final game-defined recipe prerequisites/progression target; drop weights/amounts; primary hero versus Echo eligibility; and whether a measured shortage warrants an explicitly approved fallback. Recommend matching crop packs for predictable targeting, computed recipe access from pack discovery and canonical current level/quest gates, no automatic sale/exchange or arbitrary stock cap. Preserve surplus packs as finite stock; duplicate packs remain useful through consumption. A seed pack for a higher locked crop can be retained and labelled with its level requirement if such drops are approved, never silently lost. No pity/guarantee/token schema should be implemented from this superseded version.

## Capacity, durations and crop choice

The following tables are **continuous-active-play scenarios**: modelled elapsed hours coincide with active adventure hours. For a single recipe, optimistic throughput is `min(bed capacity / growth hours, useful seed packs per active adventure hour) × batch yield`. It assumes prompt town harvest/replant with negligible interruption; actual town-command restrictions, absence, mixed recipes and unusable packs lower output. Seeds require active adventure time, whereas growth uses elapsed time including town/offline time.

Illustrative tier1 yield10; quick growth30minutes, long growth2hours; seed income1/4/12 useful packs per active hour:

| Beds | Quick: low / steady / high units/hour | Long: low / steady / high units/hour | One unattended planting |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 10 / 40 / 40 | 10 / 10 / 10 | 20 |
| 4 | 10 / 40 / 80 | 10 / 20 / 20 | 40 |
| 6 | 10 / 40 / 120 | 10 / 30 / 30 | 60 |
| 8 | 10 / 40 / 120 | 10 / 40 / 40 | 80 |

These inputs are scenario assumptions, not ordinary-run observations. Eight beds do not increase quick throughput at twelve packs/hour, but increase long-growth throughput and unattended reserves. Six is a visual/scope preference: A fits a smaller final enclosure, whereas B needs more clearing and management. It is not an economic optimum proved by these numbers. Two is a useful introduction; four is a viable smaller expansion; six completes the three-region town story; eight can be a later capacity addition if measured demand warrants it.

Ordinary-return telemetry was sought. Existing fresh profiles forced map access, midgame profiles explicitly use synthetic data, and a developed Farmlands capture runs at 4×speed with developed buffs. They cannot ground ordinary-return estimates. Authored Radish is a two-second task with a1–5 drop; DropResolver squares its random draw, giving expected2.2513 units, not a uniform mean3. This gives an expected isolated-task rate of 67.54 units/minute at authored duration, excluding travel, availability, speed and bonuses; it is not an upper bound or ordinary-player pacing. See [Radish task](../../../Assets/Resources/Tasks/Farming/Radish.asset), [DropResolver](../../../Assets/Scripts/Upgrades/DropResolver.cs), [synthetic profile disclaimer](../../profiling/2026-09-26/map-Farmlands-mid-proxy-profile.json).

Crop choice should serve specific quest/resource shortages, rather than maximizing stew alone. Prototype quick Radish at 30 minutes and compare a long recipe at 2 hours. Set per-recipe yields against crop-specific quest demand and a common stew-value budget, using actual resource points, so a late expensive crop does not dominate all alternatives. A candidate long batch offers approximately three quick batches' value for one pack, while taking four times the duration: seed scarcity favors it; frequent returns and spare packs favor quick batches. This is a testable trade-off, not a promised production recipe. At equal crop-value budget, the candidate long batch has three times the baseline table's yield value, so its throughput/reserves must also multiply by three:

| Beds | Candidate long: low / steady / high Radish-equivalent value/hour | One unattended long planting value |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 30 / 30 / 30 | 60 |
| 4 | 30 / 60 / 60 | 120 |
| 6 | 30 / 90 / 90 | 180 |
| 8 | 30 / 120 / 120 | 240 |

These are normalized resource/stew-value comparisons, not literal quantities of every crop. At six beds/high seed income quick output120 beats long 90, while scarce seeds favor long 30 versus quick 10. At eight beds/high income they tie 120; quick does not strictly dominate there. Specific quest demand and return frequency still matter. Keep the current game-defined recipe/level requirements explicit and give no repeatable farm XP. Measure actual tier bonuses and farm stew income before approving the yield table.

### Duty cycle: adventure income versus elapsed growth

For adventure duty fraction `f = active adventure hours / elapsed hours`, an optimistic elapsed-hour bound is `min(beds / growth hours, f × seed income per active adventure hour) × yield`. The continuous-active tables use `f = 1`. Harvest/replant windows and bed idle time can reduce the bound further; offline growth cannot manufacture the seeds assumed by an active-hour income rate.

Example using unmeasured scenario inputs: one active adventure hour followed by three town/offline hours, repeating, gives `f = 0.25`. At four useful packs per adventure hour, only four packs arrive per four-hour cycle: one per elapsed hour. With six beds, a baseline 10-unit yield and either 30-minute or 2-hour growth, the seed-limited bound is 10 units per elapsed hour, or 40 units per cycle, compared with the tables' continuous 40 quick/30 long units per hour. Planting those four packs on return and harvesting at the next visit can realize that cycle after startup; unattended plants produce once, then wait. The candidate long recipe with three times the value instead gives 120 normalized value per cycle, or 30 per elapsed hour. These remain scenario calculations, not observed player output.

## Proposed transaction owner and failure semantics

Extend Oracle's existing active-save/coalescing coordinator. FarmService owns pure growth/command validation; it never starts a parallel writer. SaveManager already synchronously captures immutable bytes and rejects stale heads. That protects writes, not mutable gameplay contributors.

Admit planting, harvesting, build and any explicitly approved legacy-claim commands on the main thread with operation ID, slot/tree identity, expected batch IDs and typed field deltas. Enter a short economic barrier and wait for Oracle's prior write. Coalesce autosaves. Capture existing contributors, clone the complete tree, aggregate/validate costs and apply the command to the candidate. Commit through Oracle's save lane. After confirmed authority success, patch only affected farm/inventory/quest fields and their ResourceManager caches into the same live tree; emit one notification after the full delta. Never replace the whole live tree during an asynchronous runtime command.

Use an in-memory coordinator journal for operation ID, expected ownership, touched fields, prior/proposed values and pending state. Store final claim/batch identities and final state inside the successful snapshot; no second sidecar durability protocol. Failed writes leave the candidate unapplied. Ambiguous authority results enter existing recovery; a generation file merely existing is insufficient proof of success. A publication failure after a confirmed commit requires reload recovery, not reversal of the durable payout.

First-release planting/harvesting/build hand-ins are town-only. Growth continues on adventures. Prove adventure shutdown and pending reward drainage before admission. Queue whole quest/Cauldron/crafting/conversion operations during the barrier, never just individual Spend calls; current callers can ignore Spend failures. If any adventure reward can cross the barrier, retain its run/task attribution and defer that run's finalization until rewards drain. Otherwise BestPerMinute could finalize before queued grants arrive. Defer user-initiated slot/import/reset transitions until resolution. External focus loss, pause or quit cannot simply be deferred: extend Oracle's unified blocking lifecycle drain to both confirm the farm write **and synchronously publish its typed delta/cache updates before any contributor capture or lifecycle snapshot**. Current CompleteActiveSaveBlocking handles write status only, so an unchanged SaveNowBlocking could overwrite a committed candidate with old live values. If confirmation/publication cannot complete, enter recovery and never capture/write that old economic state. Reload reads the authoritative candidate; no second payout replay occurs. Unrelated progress can continue only if narrow publication cannot overwrite it; follow with a fresh coalesced snapshot.

Recommend atomic Harvest Ready for six beds. Capture a sorted ready-batch set, validate all recipes, aggregate resource yields, apply tier bonuses once and commit all payouts/emptied beds/claim identities together. A later readiness transition stays for the next action. An unresolved selected recipe blocks the whole command and names the bed; any partial-harvest option must be explicit. A crash after commit but before UI publication reloads already-final inventory and empty beds; it never replays Add. These APIs and gates are proposed work, not capabilities that currently exist.

## Clocks, repeated advances and offline limits

Running growth uses monotonic unscaled elapsed time, ignoring wall jumps. Load/resume has one reconciliation owner: validate saved UTC, apply elapsed only to batches planted at the saved boundary, clamp to remaining duration, discard surplus and persist reconciled progress/baseline once. Foreground callbacks cannot consume the same interval twice. New plantings baseline at the reconciled current time and inherit no old absence. Backward/invalid clocks give zero offline progress and rebase; online growth continues without crop loss.

Repeated artificial forward advances across successive paid batches can accelerate stored packs. Finite batches alone do not prevent that. Recommended proportionate local/offline policy accepts this limitation: no server requirement or crop confiscation, and no false anti-cheat promise. The bound is the actual finite held seed stock plus already planted batches; there is no approved twelve-pack cap or pending guaranteed entitlement. With 100 held packs, deliberate repeated jumps could accelerate 100 paid future batches. They still debit a pack and pay once. This is a material limitation, not a claim that six beds prevent manipulation. Farm harvest does not roll seed drops, grant planting credit, auto-replant or carry unused offline elapsed time into a new batch. New supply requires actual eligible adventure plant rewards.

If stronger clock policing is desired, consider a separately reviewed suspicious-clock mode based on verified cross-session monotonic evidence. A local wall-clock-only cap would also penalize legitimate repeated offline visits, so it is not the default. Tests must cover rollback, rebasing, repeated advances and process restarts on Mac/Windows; authentication is outside this update.
